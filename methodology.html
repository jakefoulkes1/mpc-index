Methodology

How the index is built and evaluated. Every choice is also recorded, dated and forward-only, in the decisions log; dates in brackets below point at the governing entry.

Data Sample sizes Index construction Market benchmark Evaluation Limitations

Data

Every MPC minutes publication since August 2015 — 95 documents — together with the Bank's own voting record, reconciled against the Bank's sitemap rather than assumed complete.

More detail

Text corpus

Every Monetary Policy Summary and minutes publication of the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee from August 2015 to July 2026: 95 documents — 92 from the regular meeting cycle plus 2 special (emergency) meetings, the 10 March and 19 March 2020 Covid-19 decisions. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — corpus audit: full-text repair + special meetings; 2026-07-11 — 19 March 2020 special meeting added.)

The window starts at the August 2015 regime change: from that meeting the Bank began publishing the policy summary and minutes on the day of the decision (“Super Thursday”). Meetings were monthly from 2015 until the autumn of 2016, not 2017: the Bank confirmed the move to eight regular meetings a year on 9 May 2016, once the Bank of England and Financial Services Act 2016 had received Royal Assent, and the meeting scheduled for 13 October 2016 was “the first to be dropped under the new arrangements”. 2017 is therefore the first full calendar year with eight, and this corpus holds 11 documents for 2016 and 8 for 2017. The scraper probes every month and tolerates the months with no meeting rather than hardcoding the schedule. (Bank of England, Confirmation of changes to the Monetary Policy Committee meeting schedule, 9 May 2016, and MPC publication dates for 2016 and provisional dates for 2017, 24 September 2015; pipeline/scrape/era.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — era corpus; 2026-07-30 — audit fixes.)

Until August 2021 the Bank published the full minutes as a separate PDF, with only the summary on the HTML page; 52 of the 95 documents were therefore reconstructed from the Bank's own PDF minutes, and every document records whether its text came from HTML or PDF. (pipeline/scrape/backfill_pdf.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — corpus audit: full-text repair + special meetings.)

The corpus was reconciled against the Bank's own sitemap (zero missing, zero extra within the window) and against the voting record. A document's published date is meeting_end + 1 day, applied uniformly; the five known one-day gaps against the Bank's voting sheet are individually logged, and joins between the two sources use a ±3-day tolerance. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — corpus audit: full-text repair + special meetings; 2026-07-11 — validation join fixed: voting-sheet date is canonical.)

Raw source texts are © Bank of England and stay in a local, gitignored cache; the repository publishes derived scores and SHA-256 hashes only, with links to the source pages. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-05 — repo created.)

Voting record

The Bank's published MPC voting spreadsheet, parsed to one row per (meeting, member): each member's preferred Bank Rate against the decided rate. skew is the average of all voting members' preferred rates minus the decided rate, following Apel & Blix Grimaldi (2012, p.13, after Gerlach-Kristen 2004). The sheet's date column is the announcement date, confirmed against known meetings. (pipeline/build_votes.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — voting-record parsing and reconciliation.)

Why the sample sizes differ

Five different n's appear across this site, and they are not inconsistencies. The 95 above is the whole corpus: every MPC minutes publication from August 2015 to July 2026, including the two emergency meetings of March 2020.

  • 63 evaluated. The benchmark ladder scores only meetings from 1 January 2019 onwards; everything earlier is training data for the expanding window and is never scored. That leaves 63 meetings inside the evaluation window.
  • 61 scheduled. Of those 63, the 2 March 2020 emergency meetings are scored separately rather than blended in, because no call could have been locked for a meeting that had not yet been announced. The headline ladder figures are the remaining 61.
  • 92 in Spec 3. The regression uses the whole corpus rather than the 2019 window, and its dependent variable is the rate surprise. That drops the 2 specials, and August 2015 as well — the first document in the corpus has no preceding decision to measure a change from. 95 − 2 − 1 = 92.
  • 24 in the fragility check. The post-hiking-cycle subsample is those of the 92 falling on or after 1 September 2023.

So the ladder and Spec 3 answer different questions over deliberately different windows, and neither is a subset of the other by accident. (data/ladder_v1.json; data/inference_v1.json; pipeline/build_surprises.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — the benchmark ladder, L0–L4; 2026-07-11 — the headline inference; 2026-07-30 — audit fixes.)

Index construction

Each document is scored with a published 2012 dictionary of hawkish and dovish word pairs, producing one number on a 0–2 scale where 1.0 is neutral.

More detail

The index is Apel & Blix Grimaldi (2012), “The Information Content of Central Bank Minutes”, Sveriges Riksbank Working Paper No. 261, implemented as described in the paper from a retrieved copy (not reconstructed from memory); the verbatim term lists and page references are stored in pipeline/score/lexicon/abg_2012.json. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — A&BG (2012) baseline lexicon.)

Net Index = [ (#hawk / (#hawk + #dove)) − (#dove / (#hawk + #dove)) ] + 1   (paper p.10)

Range [0, 2]; 1.0 is neutral (the ratio's fixed midpoint, not a fitted value). A document with zero hits is defined as neutral. (pipeline/score/abg.py NEUTRAL_VALUE; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — neutral_value published in index.json.)

Counting rule, per the paper: the whole document is lowercased and tokenised (word tokens; no sentence splitting — the paper counts across the full text). Each adjacent adjective-then-noun pair (“higher inflation”) counts as one hawkish or dovish hit if the adjective matches one of the paper's stems by prefix; multi-word nouns (“oil price”, “cyclical position”) match as a following token sequence. The seven core nouns of the paper's main Net Index are used; the paper's four-noun robustness extension is stored but unused. Immediate adjacency in the adjective-then-noun direction is an interpretation of the paper's worked examples, logged as such. There is no negation handling — the paper has none. (pipeline/score/abg.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — A&BG (2012) baseline lexicon.)

Lag convention: a meeting's minutes are published simultaneously with its own decision, so in every predictive use (the benchmark ladder's L3 and the Spec 2/Spec 3 tests) the tone feature for a meeting is the previous meeting's index. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — the benchmark ladder, L0–L4; 2026-07-11 — the headline inference.)

Market benchmark

The bar to clear is what the market already prices: the Bank's own OIS forward curve, read three days after each meeting and mapped to cut, hold and hike probabilities.

More detail

Sources. The Bank of England's published instantaneous OIS forward curve (the daily archive oisddata.zip, whose era files cover 2009–2015 / 2016–2024 / 2025–present, plus the separate “latest yield curve data” file updated daily — whichever is fresher wins), and SONIA from the Bank's Interactive Statistical Database (series IUDSOIA). (pipeline/market/ois.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — market benchmark: OIS forward curve + SONIA; 2026-07-11 — curve freshness fix.)

Implied change. The forward rate is read from the curve at meeting date + 3 days, linearly interpolated between the curve's whole-month maturity buckets, minus SONIA. The +3-day convention compensates for the fitted curve smoothing over the discrete jump that happens at a meeting; the bias was quantified before the convention was chosen, and a 2-week-average alternative was rejected because it would overlap the next meeting in the monthly-meeting era. One implementation serves the live call card and the historical backtest. (pipeline/predict/market_probs.py, LOCK_OFFSET_DAYS=3; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — smoothed-curve bias: quantified, offset convention chosen.)

Two-state ±25bp mapping. The market is assumed to price a single possible move in one direction, of fixed 25bp size: phike or pcut = clip(|implied change| / 25, 0, 1) in the direction of the implied change, remainder phold. A documented simplification — real market pricing can imply multi-directional or non-25bp moves. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — market_probs: two-state ±25bp assumption.)

Historical benchmark. For each of the 95 meetings, the lock date is announcement − 2 calendar days, walked back day-by-day to the nearest available trading day (capped at 10 days, else a hard stop; in practice zero walk-backs occurred). Output: data/market_history.csv. (pipeline/market/ois_history.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — historical market benchmark.)

Freshness guard. A live lock refuses to write if the curve snapshot is more than 2 business days old. (pipeline/predict/lock.py assert_curve_is_fresh; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — curve freshness fix.)

Evaluation

Five forecasters are scored against each other on scheduled meetings only, each fitted on strictly earlier data, with the market-only model as the bar to beat.

More detail

Benchmark ladder (L0–L4)

  • L0 — always hold (phold = 1), the naive baseline.
  • L1 — market-only: the two-state ±25bp mapping above, exactly as in data/market_history.csv. This is the bar every other model must beat.
  • L2 — ordered logit (proportional odds, 3 classes) on the market-implied change alone.
  • L3 — ordered logit on the market-implied change plus the previous meeting's tone index and vote skew (both lagged; see the lag convention above).
  • L4 — member-level simulation: a pooled dissent-state transition matrix (fit on strictly prior votes) blended 0.5/0.5 with the market-implied distribution per member, aggregated over 5,000 Monte Carlo committee draws (fixed seed 42).

Expanding window: for each evaluated meeting, models are fit on strictly prior meetings only, training from August 2015; evaluation runs from 1 January 2019 (63 meetings). Outcomes are coded by sign only — {cut, hold, hike} — with one shared classifier (the verb adjacent to “Bank Rate” in the decision text). (pipeline/ladder.py; pipeline/decision_label.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — the benchmark ladder, L0–L4.)

Scheduled-only rule. Headline scores use the 61 scheduled meetings only: at lock time (2 business days before an announcement) nobody could have pre-registered a forecast for an emergency meeting not yet known to exist. The 2 specials are scored separately as a robustness line, never blended in. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — scheduled vs special meetings split; probability-clip logged.)

Scoring rules. Three-class Brier score (sum of squared probability errors across {cut, hold, hike}; range [0, 2]) and log score −log p(actual outcome), with p floored at the clip constant 1e‑9 to avoid −log(0) for a confidently wrong call; the clip is published in data/ladder_v1.json as log_score_probability_clip. (pipeline/predict/score_outcomes.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — lock and scoring machinery; 2026-07-11 — scheduled vs special meetings split; probability-clip logged.)

Fallbacks. An ordered-logit window with under 10 training examples, fewer than 2 outcome classes, or a non-converged fit (two-part check, including a degenerate-parameter guard) falls back to L1's probabilities and is logged in data/ladder_v1.json's fallback_log — 11 of 62 L3 windows, all before the sample's first cut in March 2020. (pipeline/predict/ordered_logit.py; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — scipy added, for the ordered logit models only.)

Statistical tests (Spec 2 and Spec 3)

Spec 3: OLS of the market surprise (actual minus market-implied rate change, basis points; data/surprises.csv) on the lagged tone index, Newey–West HAC standard errors with 4 lags, on the 91 scheduled meetings; also run with lagged skew added, and on a post-hiking-cycle fragility subsample (from 1 September 2023, n=23). Spec 2: a likelihood-ratio test of whether adding the lagged index improves an ordered logit of the three-class decision over the market-implied change alone, with a minimum-sample guard of 10 observations. (pipeline/inference.py → data/inference_v1.json; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — the headline inference (Spec 3 OLS + Spec 2 ordered-logit LR test).)

Lock-and-score protocol

Before an announcement, pipeline/predict/lock.py freezes the market-only reference and the current index readings with the git commit hash and SHA-256 hashes of every input used; a human then fills in the point call and rationale, and the committed data/predictions/lock-* file is the pre-registered record — never modified afterwards, misses included. Locks refuse stale curve data (see the freshness guard above). (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — lock and scoring machinery.)

Limitations

What could undermine the result: very few dictionary hits per document, a 2012 word list, risk premia inside the forward curve, and a small sample.

More detail
  • Risk premia in forwards. OIS forward rates embed risk/term premia as well as rate expectations, so the market-implied probabilities are not pure forecasts. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — market benchmark: OIS forward curve + SONIA.)
  • Sparse lexicon hits. Across the 95 documents, the median number of A&BG bigram hits per document (hawkish + dovish) is 2, with an interquartile range of 2 (Q1 = 1, Q3 = 3; min 0, max 12) — each document's index value rests on very few matches. (Computed read-only by pipeline/lexicon_sparsity.py from data/index.json; quartiles use the median-inclusive convention.)
  • Lexicon vintage and topic-vs-stance. The term lists are the paper's 2012 vintage, used unmodified; a match such as “higher inflation” reflects what the Committee discussed, which need not be the stance it took. (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — A&BG (2012) baseline lexicon.)
  • No negation handling. Per the paper, a phrase like “no case for higher rates” still counts its inner bigram; the paper's method has no negation rule and none was added. (pipeline/score/abg.py docstring.)
  • Smoothed-curve bias. The Bank's fitted OIS curve smooths over the discrete jump at a meeting date; the +3-day convention recovers only part of the understated move (quantified at up to a few tenths of a basis point on the 2026 curve, expected larger on steep historical curves). (DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — smoothed-curve bias: quantified, offset convention chosen.)
  • Small n. 95 documents in total; 61 scheduled meetings in the ladder's evaluation window; 23 in the fragility subsample. (data/ladder_v1.json; data/inference_v1.json.)
  • The two specifications disagree. Spec 3 (OLS on the continuous surprise) is nominally significant on the full sample (p = 0.0316) but does not replicate post-September-2023 (p = 0.1422); Spec 2 (ordered logit on the discrete decision) finds nothing (p = 0.4918). Both are reported. (data/inference_v1.json; DECISIONS.md 2026-07-11 — the headline inference.)
