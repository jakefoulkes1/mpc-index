MPC Communication Index

Does the tone of Bank of England communication carry information about the next rate decision beyond what markets already price? Every call is locked before the announcement, timestamped, and scored — including the misses.

What is this?

This project tests whether the tone of the Bank of England's MPC minutes says anything about the next Bank Rate decision that financial markets have not already priced in. Every minutes document since August 2015 is scored with a published hawkish/dovish dictionary (Apel & Blix Grimaldi, 2012), producing the communication index charted below. The bar to clear is the market itself: probabilities implied by the Bank of England's own OIS forward curve. Before each announcement a probabilistic call is locked and timestamped; after the announcement it is scored, and misses stay on the record permanently. A null result — the market already prices whatever the tone says — is itself the finding.

The communication index is recorded as context and does not enter the call: L3 scores −0.6729 against the market-only benchmark and Spec 2 returns p = 0.4918, so there is no warrant on this project's own evidence for letting tone override L1. p(hike) is shaded modestly below m0's 3.86% for two regime-specific reasons. First, with the short curve steeply upward-sloping (near 4.25% priced within six months) a forward read, at meeting plus three days, interpolated across whole-month buckets, absorbs September's expectations along with July's and overstates the probability attaching to this meeting. Second, the fiscal repricing of 20 July, which carried the ten-year gilt to 5.04%, is a risk premium rather than an expectation of Bank Rate — the contamination this methodology's first limitation anticipates. Both claims are testable against the September lock, when the near-month slope will differ. The macro flow since 18 June runs against the two dissenters on nearly every measure they invoked. Headline CPI fell to 2.6% against a 2.7% consensus, its lowest in fifteen months; services eased to 3.6%; regular pay growth slowed to 3.4%, joint-slowest since October 2020; input producer prices decelerated to 7.3% from 9.3% as Brent retraced. Greene and Pill dissented on an energy shock now unwinding. The counter-observation, recorded rather than passed over, is services producer price inflation at 4.3% in Q2, up from 3.2%.

Results

Benchmark ladder (ladder-v1)

In plain English: the market's own pricing (L1) called these decisions best — every model that adds the tone index scored worse, not better.

This table pits five forecasters against one another over every scheduled MPC decision since 1 January 2019, each restricted to information that existed before the meeting it is forecasting. Scheduled meetings only: the two emergency meetings of March 2020 are reported separately rather than blended in, because no forecast could have been pre-registered for a meeting that had not yet been announced. Lower Brier and log scores are better, and skill is measured against L1, the market-implied benchmark — not against L0, since beating always-hold is trivial and any model that cannot clear L1 has added nothing. The finding is that no text-augmented model beat market pricing out-of-sample — L3 comes in at −0.6729 against L1 — which offers no warrant for letting the tone index override what the curve already implies.

Why the sample sizes differ. The corpus is 94 documents (August 2015 – June 2026, including the two emergency meetings of March 2020). The ladder evaluates from 1 January 2019 onwards, leaving everything earlier as training data only — 62 meetings, of which the 60 scheduled ones carry the headline scores above and the 2 specials are scored separately. The Spec 3 test below runs on the whole corpus rather than the 2019 window, and on rate surprises, so it drops the 2 specials and August 2015 (no preceding decision to measure a change from): 91. The fragility subsample is the 23 of those 91 falling on or after 1 September 2023. Full note in the methodology.

More detail

Scheduled meetings only, evaluated 1 January 2019 → present (n=60) · 2 special meeting(s) reported separately, not blended in · log-score probability floor 1e-09.

Standing caveat: the market benchmark (L1) is the bar. L2–L4 must show genuine skill above L1 to be worth anything — matching or beating L0 (always-hold) is not the test, since L1 already does that easily. See the methodology log for every modelling choice and the full per-meeting breakdown in data/ladder_v1.json.

Headline statistical test (Spec 3)

In plain English: one test finds a weak link between the previous minutes' tone and how the decision surprised the market, but it fades after 2023 and a stricter test finds nothing.

More detail

Regressing each meeting's market surprise on the previous meeting's index (OLS, Newey–West standard errors, 4 lags, n=91 scheduled meetings): coefficient -2.163349, t = -2.1499, p = 0.0316. On the post-hiking-cycle subsample (from 1 September 2023, n=23) the result does not replicate: coefficient -3.441919, p = 0.1422. Spec 2, an ordered-logit likelihood-ratio test on the discrete decision, finds nothing: LR = 0.4726, p = 0.4918. Full output: data/inference_v1.json.
